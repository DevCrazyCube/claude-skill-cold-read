# Surprise read: a 10-second reel pitch, "The library at 9:00"

**Reader:** a fresh agent, used once · **Run:** 1 · The answers are the reader's own words, unedited.

## Pass criteria (written before the read)

- The situation it names is: seats in a library held by coats whose owners are gone.
- None of its three guesses is: the frame widens and the whole hall is coats, the student the only person in it.
- The reason it gives is: everyone reserves a seat and nobody uses it.
- It would pass it on, to a kind of person.

## Message 1 (the setup, as sent)

> This is the start of a 10-second silent cartoon reel for students.
>
> Text on screen at 0.0 s: "The library at 9:00"
>
> What we see: A student with a laptop walks along a row of desks. One chair has a coat on it and nobody in it.
> They look at the next chair: a coat. And the next.
>
> 1. What is the situation, in one sentence?
> 2. What happens next? Give your three most likely guesses, numbered, one sentence each.

**Answer:**

> 1. A student arrives at the library at 9:00 looking for a desk, but every seat is already "reserved" by a coat
>    with no person in it.
>
> 2. Guesses:
>    1. The camera pulls back to show the whole library full of coats and not a single person studying.
>    2. The student gives up and sits on the floor (or stairs, or by the toilets) with the laptop.
>    3. The student finally finds one free chair, then puts their own coat on it and walks off for coffee,
>       becoming one of them.

## Message 2 (the turn, as sent)

> What actually happens next: the frame widens. The whole hall is coats on chairs, and the student is the only
> person in it. The student stands still in the middle, laptop under one arm, looking at the camera.
>
> 1. Why did that happen, in one sentence?
> 2. Would a student pass this reel on to someone? Answer yes, no or maybe, and if yes, to what kind of person?

**Answer:**

> 1. Everyone came in early, claimed a desk with their coat and left again, so the library is "full" without
>    anyone actually studying there.
>
> 2. Maybe, leaning yes: to the friend who always drops their coat on a chair at 8:30 and vanishes for coffee, or
>    to the study-group chat during exam weeks. The truth is recognisable, but the widening only confirms what the
>    first three coats already told me (it was my first guess), so it lands as a nod more than a laugh.

## Verdict

| Check | Result | Note |
|---|---|---|
| The situation | pass | It named the situation exactly |
| The three guesses | **fail** | Guess 1 is the turn, almost word for word |
| The reason | pass | Unprompted, and the intended one |
| Passing it on | fail | "Maybe, leaning yes" is not a yes |

**Stopped.** The setup is clear and the truth is recognised; the ending only illustrates it.

What changes: the truth stays, the turn goes. Guess 3 (the student finds the one free chair, puts their own coat
on it and walks off) is a better turn than the author's: it is something the character does, and it makes the
student part of the problem. It becomes the next pitch, and that pitch goes to a new reader.

The whole read took two messages.
